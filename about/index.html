About MartechSignal

MartechSignal is an independent review site for AI marketing automation. We research and document the tools in your stack: workflow automation, CRM, email, analytics, SEO, content AI, and publish what we find, including what vendors would rather leave out of their landing pages.

Who runs this

MartechSignal is written and maintained by Tim Christensen, a marketing-operations practitioner who has spent years building and breaking martech stacks for a living. No agency writes this. No vendor previews these reviews before publication. The work behind each page is source-reading: vendor docs, pricing pages, release notes, and repository activity, checked against each other before anything gets published.

What the directory covers

Today the directory holds 160 active tool records across 14 categories (162 records in the catalog, two of them retired), plus side-by-side comparison pages, an alternatives series, a glossary, and a blog that follows the market between reviews. Two pieces of infrastructure shape how this site treats machines: the catalog in plain JSON for agent readers, and an AI policy page spelling out which crawlers are welcome. Both exist for the same reason. A review site should be readable by the tools the reviews are about.

How we evaluate tools

Every tool page is built from a structured audit: pricing model and real entry cost, self-hosting options, API availability, integration surface, AI features that actually ship versus demo-ware, and community signals such as G2 ratings and GitHub activity where they exist. Where a tool excels or falls short, the review says so plainly.

How verification dates work

Every tool page carries the date its facts were last checked. Prices get a rolling window: anything with a price on it is re-verified within 21 days, and a page that passes the window without a check gets flagged in the build and shows a note on the page itself. When a re-check finds drift, the page changes and the old number leaves. Where a price cannot be confirmed from the vendor's own pages, the number is left out rather than guessed.

Pages verified by hand

A short list beats a long promise. The claude-seo review carries a dated hands-on block from real runs of the tool against this site: a 170-page audit that surfaced 96 findings in about five minutes at roughly six dollars of API tokens per run. The benchmark post under /blog/ records the same work with the method attached. Everything else in the directory is labeled desk research on its face.

Corrections and the record

When a published claim turns out wrong, the fix is not silent. The correction lands on /corrections/ with the date it was found and the date it was fixed, and the affected page changes in the same pass. Price drift is the common case: a vendor moves a number and a review keeps quoting the old one. That is what the 21-day window is for, and every page says when its numbers were last checked so you can judge the rest.

Suggestions and challenges are welcome from vendors as well as readers. They get the same treatment either way: check the source, fix the page, note the correction. Nobody gets a preview.

The standing promises

  • No sponsored rankings. Placement in the directory is not for sale. Vendors cannot pay to improve their position or rating.
  • No affiliate links. When you see "Visit site," it goes straight to the vendor. We earn nothing on the click.
  • How we make money. We do not, yet. The site is self-funded: no ads, no sponsorships, no paid placement, no affiliate income. If that changes, it will be disclosed here and on every affected page before it changes.
  • Corrections welcome. Pricing and features change fast. Spot something stale, and it gets fixed.

Editorial standards

Analysis posts are opinionated by design, but claims are grounded in what the tool actually does today, not roadmap promises. When we test a tool's AI features, we say how we tested. When a category is moving faster than reviews can track, we say that too.
