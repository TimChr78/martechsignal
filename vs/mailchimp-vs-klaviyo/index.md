## Mailchimp vs Klaviyo (2026): pricing, ecommerce depth, verdict

Pick Mailchimp if you want broad channel coverage with a free plan to 500 contacts. Pick Klaviyo if you run an ecommerce store and want predictive flows, free to 250 contacts.

Mailchimp and Klaviyo end up on the same shortlist whenever a store outgrows newsletters. Mailchimp is the most recognized name in email marketing, with over 11 million users from solopreneurs to mid-market, and an Intuit-owned platform that now spans landing pages, ads, SMS, automation, and a basic CRM. Klaviyo is the dominant email and SMS platform for ecommerce brands, built around behavioral profiles from purchase history, browsing, and predicted lifetime value.

Most decisions here come down to how it bills. The figures below are the catalog's last verified numbers, each dated on the tool page. This page is desk research rather than a hands-on test.

The choice usually lands on one question: is the store the center of the data model or one channel among several. Klaviyo assumes commerce data is the foundation and prices by contacts. Mailchimp assumes a mixed audience and prices by plan tier, which stays cheap for small lists and climbs steeply once ecommerce volume arrives.

## Positioning

**Mailchimp:** Mailchimp is the most recognized name in email marketing, serving over 11 million users from solopreneurs to mid-market businesses. Founded in 2001 in Atlanta and acquired by Intuit in 2021 for $12 billion, it has expanded into landing pages, social ads, postcards, SMS, automation, and a basic CRM.

**Klaviyo:** Klaviyo is the dominant email and SMS marketing platform for ecommerce brands, built from the ground up around behavioral data and deep integrations with Shopify, WooCommerce, BigCommerce, and Magento. Founded in 2012 in Boston and taken public in 2023.

## Pricing

**Mailchimp:** Free plan (500 contacts, 1,000 emails/mo); Essentials $13/mo; Standard $20/mo; Premium $350/mo.

**Klaviyo:** Free up to 250 contacts/500 emails/mo; paid scales with contact count from ~$20/mo.

## Deployment and self-hosting

**Mailchimp:** Hosted only. The vendor runs the infrastructure and bills by plan tier and contact blocks.

**Klaviyo:** Hosted only. The vendor runs the infrastructure and bills by contact count plus channels used.

## AI features

**Mailchimp:** 5 documented AI features, including AI content optimizer, AI subject line helper, predictive demographics, AI send-time optimization, and creative assistant.

**Klaviyo:** 5 documented AI features, including AI subject line assistant, predictive analytics, AI send-time optimization, AI product recommendations, and AI content generation.

## Integrations

**Mailchimp:** 8 listed integrations, including Shopify, WooCommerce, Salesforce, Zapier, and WordPress.

**Klaviyo:** 8 listed integrations, including Shopify, WooCommerce, BigCommerce, Salesforce, and Slack.

## Lock-in and exit cost

**Mailchimp:** Mailchimp exports contacts, templates, and campaign reports. Automations rebuild elsewhere, and the Intuit bundle discount only matters while the whole stack is used.

**Klaviyo:** Klaviyo's lock-in is its data model. Profiles carry event streams that power flows and segments, and rival platforms rarely ingest them cleanly. Ecommerce teams accept this because the revenue reporting is hard to give up.

## Deliverability and sending

**Mailchimp:** Mailchimp's sending covers the general case well: list hygiene tooling, authentication defaults, and deliverability guidance aimed at mixed B2C lists. High-volume ecommerce bursts are supported but not the design center.

**Klaviyo:** Klaviyo's sending is tuned for ecommerce bursts: segmentation-heavy sends at flash-sale volume, with deliverability tooling focused on list hygiene and engagement-based throttling. Dedicated IPs come into play at scale.

## Decision notes

**Mailchimp:** Pick Mailchimp if you want broad channel coverage beyond email and a free plan up to 500 contacts, starting at $13/mo for Essentials.

**Klaviyo:** Pick Klaviyo if you want ecommerce-native profiles and predictive flows, starting free up to 250 contacts with paid from around $20/mo.

## Migration cost

Moving lists is the easy afternoon. Klaviyo's flows, segments, and predictive models rebuild from the event stream, so the Shopify or WooCommerce integration moves first and history replays into fresh profiles; template styling gets redone in the new editor.

Deliverability deserves its own line in the plan. Consent records and suppression lists have to travel with the contacts, and sending reputation does not. Warm the new setup gradually instead of importing the whole list and mailing it on day one.

Going the other way, Mailchimp imports Klaviyo lists cleanly but the behavioral segments flatten into tags and static segments. Budget a week to rebuild the three flows that earn their keep: welcome, abandoned cart, and post-purchase.

## When neither is the right answer

Neither fits a team whose core need is B2B pipeline with human follow-up: that is CRM territory. And below a few thousand contacts with one campaign a month, the free tiers overlap enough that the switch saves nothing.

## Who should pick which

- **Pick Mailchimp if:** you want broad channel coverage beyond email and a free plan up to 500 contacts, starting at $13/mo for Essentials.
- **Pick Klaviyo if:** you want ecommerce-native profiles and predictive flows, starting free up to 250 contacts with paid from around $20/mo.

## Which one fits a store, and which fits everyone else?

Klaviyo for stores where purchase data drives the messaging, with predictive analytics and product recommendations tuned to it. Mailchimp for mixed audiences that also need landing pages, ads, and postcards in one account.

## Do both run the messaging for the team?

Yes. Both are hosted platforms the vendor runs, so the buyer compares data model and channel breadth rather than infrastructure. Neither asks the team to maintain servers.

## What if the team wants to self-host instead?

Look at Listmonk for newsletter sending hosted in-house, or Mautic for automation outside the SaaS ladder. The open-source marketing tools roundup on this site prices that trade honestly.
