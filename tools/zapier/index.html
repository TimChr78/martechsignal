Zapier

No-code automation platform connecting 7,000+ apps with AI-powered workflows

Workflow Automation · Freemium

Overview

Zapier is the name everyone knows in automation, and for good reason. It connects 7,000+ apps with a clean, no-code interface that non-technical marketers can actually use on day one. Founded in 2011 and headquartered in San Francisco, it is the default choice when someone says they need to connect X to Y without writing code. For marketing teams, Zapier handles the routine stuff: form submissions to CRM, new leads to Slack, event registrations to email sequences. It added AI capabilities (AI agents, chatbots, content generation) in 2024-2026, though AI steps consume tasks at accelerated rates. That task-based pricing model is the tradeoff: a 20-step workflow counts as 20 tasks, so complex automation gets expensive fast. The 7,000+ integration library is Zapier's real moat. Niche martech tools that lack native n8n nodes or Make modules almost always have a Zapier integration. For teams running simple workflows across many tools, nothing beats the breadth. But at scale, hundreds of multi-step runs per month, the per-task pricing becomes prohibitive. At that point n8n's per-execution model saves 5-10x. Zapier is best for non-technical marketing teams with straightforward automation needs, organizations with diverse martech stacks needing wide integration coverage, and teams that value ease of use over deep customization.

Hands-on notes

Zapier is the default answer when someone says they want to connect two apps without code. The model is simple to understand: trigger, steps, done, with a visual editor that any marketer can learn in an afternoon. It has tens of thousands of supported apps, so the long tail of obscure tools is covered better than anywhere else. The cost grows with usage: pricing is per active task, and chatty integrations or high-volume automations hit the top tiers fast.

The practical limits are the same ones its competitors exploit: multi-branch logic gets awkward compared to Make's graph editor, and there is no cheap way to run workflows on your own infrastructure. For a team whose automation needs are a few dozen well-chosen integrations, Zapier is still the least-effort choice on the market.

Verdict

Buy Zapier for breadth and onboarding speed. Move to Make or n8n when branching logic or volume starts making the per-task price hurt.
