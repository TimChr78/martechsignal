Monica Review

Open-source personal CRM for tracking friends, family, and business relationships

CRM · Open Source · OPEN SOURCE

Overview

Monica is an open-source personal CRM built around a single premise: remember everything about the people in your life. Contacts, conversations, reminders, journal entries, and relationship details live in one self-hostable interface, backed by a Laravel/PHP codebase with a large install base (25,000-plus GitHub stars and millions of Docker pulls). It is not a sales CRM: there are no pipelines, forecasts, or campaign tools, and treating it as one will disappoint. Where it fits a marketing context is narrow but real: founder-led and community-led businesses where relationships are the channel, since Monica is designed for exactly the follow-up discipline that newsletters and personal outreach depend on. Note the development state honestly: the main branch has been in a long-running beta toward its next major version, and the hosted monicahq.com offering is the company's revenue focus, which means self-hosters should expect to track releases and migrate deliberately. The mobile apps pair with the hosted service first. For a personal CRM it is the category's reference implementation; for team sales, look elsewhere in this category.

AI Capabilities

  • AI-assisted reminders and journaling prompts

Hands-on notes

Monica is a personal CRM - managing relationships with friends and family rather than sales pipelines. Activities, reminders to stay in touch, gift tracking, and journal-style notes about people live in one place, self-hostable via Docker or usable hosted.

It is deliberately not a business tool: no deals, no pipelines, no team-oriented sales reporting. The import path from spreadsheets is workable, and the API covers automation needs. Anyone who has lost track of important personal relationships will understand the use case immediately.

Verdict

Thoughtful personal relationship tracker; explicitly not for sales teams.
