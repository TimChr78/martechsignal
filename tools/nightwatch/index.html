Nightwatch pricing & plans

Rank tracking across Google and AI answers, priced by keyword with unlimited seats

GEO & LLM Optimization · From $79/mo

MartechSignal editorial review by Tim Christensen · updated 2026-09-25

Overview

Nightwatch is a rank tracker that prices by tracked keyword and puts its full toolkit on every plan with unlimited seats. Starter runs EUR 79/month, or EUR 948 billed yearly, for 500 keywords across 5 websites and 5 competitors per site. Professional (EUR 159/month) lifts that to 2,500 keywords and 25 websites. Agency (EUR 399/month) covers 7,500 keywords and 100 websites. Enterprise is custom from 20,000 keywords. Yearly billing is two months free across the board. Search coverage is the wide part: Google, YouTube, Bing, Yahoo, DuckDuckGo and Google Maps, with daily automated updates, real-time on-demand checks, 107,000+ locations across 190+ countries, and full SERP archives kept for three years. The AI side tracks prompts across ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, AI Mode and AI Overviews. Each plan meters AI prompts and AI answers per month (1,500 answers on Starter, 15,000 on Agency), with sentiment analysis of how answers describe your brand and citation analysis of which sources they cite. Prompt Research shows which AI prompts your brand already appears in. Six tools ship on every plan, Starter included: Content Studio, Keyword Research, Prompt Research, the SEO MCP server, Site Audit and the Search Simulator. What changes by tier is volume: keyword research runs, SEO Agent credits, and pages per site audit. Looker Studio, white-label reports and API access arrive at Professional, and a dedicated account manager plus priority support at Agency. The SEO MCP server connects Claude, Cursor or ChatGPT to live rankings on any plan. Trials run 14 days without a credit card. The company states SOC 2 compliance, with SSO on Enterprise.

Best for

Agencies and growing teams that track keywords across many sites and want Google rank data and AI answer tracking under one seat-free roof.

Not for

Solo site owners carrying more than 500 keywords on a tight budget, or teams that need deep backlink and content research rather than tracking.

Review notes

Researched from public documentation, the source repository, and vendor materials. Not a hands-on test.

Setup is keyword-first. You add domains, pick locations and languages from a large location list, choose competitors per site, and the daily updates start. On-demand checks cover anything that needs current numbers. Because the billing unit is a tracked keyword and seats are unlimited, the planning question is keyword scope, not team size. That inverts the usual agency math: adding a colleague or a client viewer costs nothing, and a growing keyword list is what moves the bill. The 14-day trial needs no credit card, but it converts to a paid monthly subscription if it is not canceled, with reminder emails sent beforehand.

The AI tracking works like a prompt-based rank tracker. You define prompts, then Nightwatch collects answers against a monthly allowance: 1,500 on Starter, 4,500 on Professional, 15,000 on Agency. Sentiment analysis scores how positively answers describe the brand, and citation analysis shows which sources the answers lean on. Prompt Research inverts the flow and finds prompts the brand already surfaces in. A practical constraint: heavy prompt tracking on Starter eats the 1,500-answer allowance fast, and brands and URLs are tracked without limits against those answer caps.

The rest of the toolkit is shared across tiers and metered separately: keyword research runs (100 to 2,500 per month), SEO Agent credits (100 to 5,000), and site audit pages (1,000 to 100,000 per crawl). Reporting is built for client work. White-label reports and Looker Studio land at Professional, and API access arrives at the same tier, with custom rate limits at Agency. Agencies are the design center here: the seat policy, the white-labeling and the SERP API all point that way.

Verdict

A rank tracker with real AI-visibility coverage and pricing that does not punish team size. Starter caps will strain busy agencies, but the upgrade ladder is honest about what changes and what does not.
