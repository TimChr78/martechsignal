Warpdrive Review

Self-hosted open-source Pipedrive alternative for BD teams: pipelines and Gmail on your own box

CRM · Open Source · OPEN SOURCE

MartechSignal editorial review · updated 2026-09-03

Overview

Warpdrive is a self-hosted CRM that reimplements Pipedrive's core business development loop: kanban pipelines, a deal workspace, contacts, and two-way Gmail. It runs on your own box under the MIT license, single-tenant, with no per-seat bill. The pitch targets small BD teams that want pipeline tracking without handing customer data to a vendor cloud, and the whole stack comes up with one docker compose command. The AI angle needs an honest answer: there isn't one. No lead scoring model, no AI email drafting, nothing in the product that learns anything. The repo ships a CLAUDE.md and a PostHog MCP config, but those help developers work on the code, not users sell. Where Warpdrive earns its place is as a clean, actively maintained open-source CRM with a real email surface: open and click tracking, templates, merge fields, scheduled send, and Gmail threads linked to deals in both directions. Pricing is the simple part. It costs nothing beyond the server you host it on. One compose file starts the app, a WebSocket server, a background worker, Postgres, MinIO, and Caddy handling TLS automatically. Set against Pipedrive's per-seat billing, the trade is plain: you swap a subscription for ops responsibility, and Google Workspace provides your SSO and Gmail OAuth. This directory carries several open-source CRMs, and Warpdrive's niche is narrowness. Twenty is the bigger project with more objects and a larger community. EspoCRM and SuiteCRM cover more business processes. Warpdrive deliberately leaves products, invoicing, forecasting, and workflow automation out of scope, and its README says so in a comparison table. What you get is the Pipedrive-style pipeline plus Gmail, polished. Skip it if you need marketing automation, forecasts, or mobile apps. The project calls those out of scope and means it. It is also one maintainer's work, in production for a single team, with 73 stars and 33 commits at review, so treat it as young software. For a solo founder or a small BD team that runs its own infrastructure and lives in Gmail, it is worth an afternoon to try.
