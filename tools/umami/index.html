Umami Review

Open-source privacy-focused web analytics with real-time dashboards and AI insights

Open-Source Tools · Open Source · OPEN SOURCE

Overview

Umami is an open-source alternative to traditional analytics platforms. It gives website owners a lightweight way to measure traffic, campaigns, and on-site behavior while keeping data collection privacy focused. The San Francisco-based project, founded in 2020 and backed by an active GitHub community with about 37,900 stars, can be self-hosted under the MIT license or used through paid cloud plans. Its real-time dashboards track pageviews, sessions, referrers, countries, devices, and custom events, while an API and integrations with WordPress, Next.js, Vercel, Zapier, Slack, and Google Search Console make it practical for marketing teams, indie builders, agencies, and product-led organizations that want simple reporting without complex setup. Umami's AI features are designed to reduce manual analysis by surfacing AI-powered insights, flagging unusual traffic patterns through AI anomaly detection, and supporting AI event tracking for more structured measurement. These capabilities are useful for teams that need quick signals from their web data but do not want to manage a full enterprise analytics stack. Compared with commercial alternatives such as Google Analytics or Mixpanel, Umami trades some depth of behavioral modeling and advertising ecosystem integrations for transparency, self-hosting control, and a cleaner privacy posture. Pricing includes a free self-hosted option, a Cloud Hobby tier at $20 per month, and higher Pro and Enterprise plans for teams needing managed infrastructure and support. It fits privacy-conscious marketers, developers, and small to mid-sized teams that want an open-source, AI-assisted web analytics tool with straightforward dashboards and flexible deployment options.

Hands-on notes

Umami is the open-source analytics answer when Plausible-style simplicity must live on your own infrastructure: one Node app plus a database, cookieless tracking, and a clean single-page dashboard covering pageviews, referrers, and events. Setup from Docker to first data took minutes.

Event tracking and custom dashboards are more limited than Matomo, and there is no commercial support layer unless you pay for their cloud. For sites wanting privacy-friendly, self-hosted numbers without operational weight, it is the lightest credible option we have deployed.

Verdict

The lightest credible self-hosted analytics; ideal when Plausible's subscription is the objection.
