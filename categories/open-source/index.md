## Open-Source Tools

The self-hosted martech stack: every open-source tool in the directory, across CRM, analytics, email, content, and automation

This category is the meta view: every open-source tool in the directory, seventy-nine across thirteen categories, listed together as the self-hosted martech stack. Assembled layer by layer, the stack is real: Ghost or Strapi publish content, Listmonk and BillionMail send email, Umami, Plausible, and PostHog measure it, Twenty and Frappe CRM hold the pipeline, n8n automates the glue, and Chatwoot answers the conversation. Some of these carry serious gravity: n8n&#x27;s repository shows over 200,000 stars, Strapi&#x27;s over 70,000, Ghost&#x27;s over 55,000.

The failure mode is reading the license price as the total price. Free self-hosting moves cost into operations: upgrades, backups, deliverability, moderation, and the page you get at 3am. Licenses also matter commercially. Thirty-six of the seventy-nine use MIT, but sixteen are AGPL-3.0 and five are GPL-3.0, which changes what you can wrap and ship. And open source here does not mean no vendor: most of these projects also sell a hosted edition (Ghost&#x27;s cloud starts at $9 a month, Chatwoot&#x27;s at $19, Strapi&#x27;s cloud Pro at $99), so the self-hosted tier is often the free tier of a real business.

Judge these repos the way you would a hire: upgrade discipline, API completeness, documentation, and whether the community would survive the founding company leaving. Start by replacing the layer your stack is missing or overpaying for, not the whole stack at once. The directory&#x27;s own split shows where open source is strongest: eighteen CRM systems, seventeen agent-skill packs, and eight each in workflow automation and email marketing. The buying question is never can it send email. It is who runs the server, and what breaks when nobody is watching.
